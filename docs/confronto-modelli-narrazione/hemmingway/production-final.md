# Scene: Minibar Feelings

## Narrative

> Visual: Hotel reception hall at dusk, warm brass lamps over a long marble desk. Alex, the 28-year-old adult male protagonist, has blonde hair and wears his black leather jacket as he leans on the counter with a grin. Behind the desk, an adult female receptionist in a pressed uniform rests one hand on a printed minibar card and looks amused.

Alex had checked in eleven minutes ago and was still deciding whether the hotel smelled like money or like despair, because honestly it did both, in equal, expensive measure. He dropped his keycard on the counter and folded his arms across the black leather jacket.

**Receptionist:** "One more thing before you head up. We've gone all in on the minibar. Really went all in."

She said it with such conviction, such genuine pride, that Alex felt something dangerous stir in his chest. He looked her up and down the way men do when a sentence lands softer than expected.

**Alex:** "You really did."

**Receptionist:** "Chilled champagne. Belgian chocolate. That little espresso machine people pretend not to use."

She slid a laminated card toward him, and he noticed she had underlined "all in" with a green highlighter. Like a woman marking her own handwriting for emphasis. Like a woman who cared about a sentence.

**Alex:** "You know, most receptionists just tell you the minibar charges a fee."

**Receptionist:** "And most people just don't read the card."

**Alex:** "So this is a warning or an invitation?"

He leaned in on his elbows, and for one golden second he was certain. Certain enough to risk it.

**Alex:** "Honestly, when you say you've gone all in, I feel like I'm being courted."

The receptionist blinked. She looked at the laminated card, then back at him, and her mouth did the thing that precedes a laugh someone is trying not to have in public.

**Receptionist:** "I mean the minibar. Room 409. Thirty-seven bottles of wine and a sad little vacuum-sealed bag of pretzels."

She tapped the card with one manicured nail.

**Receptionist:** "Not a personal offer. Just hospitality. Very non-romantic, actually."

Alex straightened up and studied his reflection in the marble counter, where he looked like a man who had just been gently corrected by a woman in a uniform.

**Alex:** "Thirty-seven bottles. Wow. You really do go all in on your feelings."

She laughed then, properly, and stamped the minibar card with the kind of authority only hotel staff can master.
